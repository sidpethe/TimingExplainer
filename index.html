Chapter 01

Why does a billionth of a second matter?

Modern life runs on synchronised time. Most of it is invisible — but if the world's clocks disagreed by even a few millionths of a second, many systems would quietly fail.

Navigation

GPS works by timing radio signals from satellites. Your position comes from how long those signals took to arrive.

1 ns error ≈ 30 cm

Telecoms & internet

Mobile networks hand your call between towers and pack data into precise time slots. Both need towers to agree on the time.

5G: ~1.5 µs alignment

Power grids

Grid operators time-stamp measurements across thousands of kilometres to keep alternating current in step and spot faults fast.

~1 µs time-stamps

Finance

Regulators require trades to be time-stamped accurately so the order of millions of transactions can be reconstructed.

EU rules: ≤100 µs

The GPS lesson

Each GPS satellite carries atomic clocks and continuously broadcasts the time. Your phone listens to several satellites and works out how far away each one is from the time delay of its signal.

Radio waves travel at the speed of light — about 30 centimetres every nanosecond. So every billionth of a second of clock error turns into roughly a third of a metre of position error.
