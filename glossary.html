Terminology

Glossary

Key terms in plain English.

Frequency

How many times something repeats per second, measured in hertz (Hz). 1 THz (terahertz) is a trillion hertz.

Oscillator

Anything that repeats at a steady rate — the "pendulum" of a clock. In optical clocks, it's a laser locked to atoms.

Resonance

The specific frequency at which a system responds most strongly. Atoms only absorb light at their resonant frequencies.

Fractional uncertainty

How far a clock might be off relative to its frequency. 10⁻¹⁸ means an error of one part in a billion billion.

Laser cooling

Using carefully tuned laser light to slow atoms down, chilling them to within millionths of a degree of absolute zero.

Optical lattice

A grid of light formed by overlapping laser beams, which traps atoms in a regular pattern like eggs in a carton.

Magic wavelength

A lattice laser colour chosen so that trapping the atoms shifts both clock energy levels equally, leaving the ticking rate undisturbed.

Frequency comb

A laser whose light consists of thousands of evenly spaced colours. It links optical frequencies to countable radio frequencies.

Quality factor (Q)

Resonant frequency divided by resonance width. A higher Q means a sharper, more precisely defined tick.

Gravitational redshift

The slowing of time deeper in a gravitational field. Clocks lower down tick slower than clocks higher up.

Documentation & Evidence

Sources & standards by section

Every calculation, simulation rate, and architectural diagram across this explainer is drawn from primary metrology research, international standards specifications, and published field trials.

Part 01: Why Time Matters & GPS

  • BIPM SI Base Unit Definition: The second is defined by taking the fixed numerical value of the Caesium frequency Δν_Cs, the unperturbed ground-state hyperfine transition of Caesium-133, to be 9,192,631,770 Hz (BIPM SI Brochure).
  • GPS Satellite Trilateration Physics: Distance error scales as c × Δt (speed of light 3 × 10⁸ m/s × 1 µs = 300 m position error).
  • Ashby, N. (2002): Relativity in the Global Positioning System, Physics Today, 55(5), 41–47. Documents the net +38 µs/day relativistic drift between ground clocks and satellite orbits.

Parts 02 & 03: Atoms as Tuning Forks

  • NIST Time and Frequency Division: Principles of atomic resonance, microwave interrogation cavities, and servo feedback loops in frequency standards (NIST Metrology).
  • Ramsey, N. F. (Nobel Prize in Physics 1989): Technique of separated oscillatory fields, enabling ultra-narrow spectral linewidths in atomic beam frequency standards.
  • Essen, L. & Parry, J. V. L. (1955): An Atomic Standard of Frequency and Time Interval, Nature, 176, 280–282. First operational caesium standard at NPL.

Parts 04 & 05: Optical Lattices & Combs

  • Nobel Prize in Physics (2005): John L. Hall and Theodor W. Hänsch for the optical frequency comb technique, synthesizing microwave counting and optical frequencies (Nobel Foundation).
  • Katori, H. et al. (2003) & Ye, J. (JILA): Invention of magic-wavelength optical lattice clocks, trapping neutral Strontium-87 atoms at 429,228,004,229,873 Hz without ac-Stark frequency shifts.
  • Ludlow, A. D. et al. (2015): Optical atomic clocks, Reviews of Modern Physics, 87(2), 637. Authoritative review of optical lattice and trapped-ion standards.

Part 06: Precision & Gravitational Redshift

  • Bothwell, T. et al. (2022): Resolving the gravitational redshift within a millimeter-scale atomic sample, Nature, 602, 420–424. Measured gravitational time dilation across sub-millimeter vertical separations.
  • Chou, C. W. et al. (2010): Optical Clocks and Relativity, Science, 329(5999), 1630–1633. Measured gravitational redshift between two optical clocks separated vertically by 33 cm.
  • Einstein Equivalence Principle: Fractional gravitational shift Δf / f = g Δh / c² ≈ 1.09 × 10⁻¹⁶ per metre of vertical elevation change.

Part 05 / 07: 5G Network Synchronization, GPS Jamming & Holdover

  • 3GPP TS 38.104 (§6.5.3 / §6.5.3.1): NR; Base Station (BS) radio transmission and reception — mandates |Δt| ≤ 1.5 µs absolute alignment to UTC/GNSS and 3.0 µs inter-cell alignment between adjacent TDD cells to prevent cross-link interference.
  • 3GPP TS 38.133 (§7 & §8): Requirements for support of radio resource management — specifies timing boundaries for inter-cell handovers and carrier aggregation.
  • ITU-T Recommendations G.8271 / G.8271.1: Time and phase synchronization aspects of packet networks — establishes Category A ±1.5 µs time error budgets for 5G cellular networks.
  • ITU-T G.8262.1 & G.8272 / G.8272.1: Local oscillator stability criteria and enhanced Primary Reference Time Clock (ePRTC) holdover specifications (≤ 100 ns over 14 days).
  • ATIS-0900105: GNSS Vulnerability and Mitigation in 5G Networks — failure cascade modeling under GNSS denial and holdover oscillator exhaustion.
  • Field Deployments & Hardware: Commercial specifications from Infleqtion (Tiqker rack-mount cold-atom optical clock, <1 ns/day drift), Vector Atomic (molecular iodine standards), Safran / Quantum Corridor White Rabbit dark-fiber demonstrations, and UK National Physical Laboratory (NPL) deployable atomic clocks.
